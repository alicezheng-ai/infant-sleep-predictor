Is this normal for your baby?

Tracker apps tell you what changed since yesterday. This tells you whether it is inside your baby's own range.

How these are counted

Everything here is computed from your own child's history — nothing is compared to a population average. A night is every sleep starting between 18:00 and 08:00, grouped under the date it began the evening on. Day sleep is every sleep starting between 06:00 and 18:00. The anchor is the longest single block of a night.

The band is the middle 80% of the last 21 nights — the normal range. The thin line is the median. The dot is the most recent night. Feed metrics use only the last 16 days, since logging habits drift.

A run of nights outside the band only counts as a run if they fall on the same side of it. A long night does not extend a run of short ones. Measures that swing more than 12% from night to night can go outside range without that meaning anything, so they are reported without a cause suggested.

When something changes about how a number is produced rather than about your baby — a different diaper at night, a different way of stopping the feed timer — the old range no longer applies. Those measures are held back until enough nights accumulate under the new arrangement.

Your tracker app uses different day boundaries, so its totals will not match these. Neither is wrong; they answer different questions.

This is a descriptive tool, not medical advice. It describes what is usual for one child and cannot tell you whether that is healthy. Growth, feeding adequacy, and anything that worries you belong with your pediatrician.
